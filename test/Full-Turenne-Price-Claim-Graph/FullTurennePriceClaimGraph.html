Additional notes:

Why do the scientists use a peptide substrate instead of p53-GST to compare GSK3-beta activity in growing cells to activity in G0-arrested or irradiated cells (Obs 14-16, Inf 8)?

GSK3-beta phosphorylation of p53 is being presented for the first time in this paper, and is not established in the field. As an initial test of GSK3-beta activity, they use a peptide substrate that has been well established to be phosphorylated by GSK3-beta. Enzyme activity on this substrate is likely to be accepted as valid by other researchers. After using the peptide substrate, they demonstrate the same pattern of activity using p53 as a substrate (Obs 17, Inf 9). Consistency of activity with both substrates makes the result with p53 more reliable.

Why do some of the Observations use comparison to cells arrested in G0?

Previous results indicate that GSK3-beta has its highest activity in resting cells (i.e., cells arrested in G0). GSK3-beta activity is expected to be lower in growing cells. These researchers demonstrate that their assay is working as expected by finding a higher GSK3-beta activity in arrested cells compared to growing cells. Then, they measure activity in irradiated cells and find that activity is lower than in growing cells (counter to the hypothesis that GSK3-beta phosphorylates p53 in response to irradiation). Their observation of high activity in arrested cells provides more confidence that their observation of low activity in irradiated cells is valid.
